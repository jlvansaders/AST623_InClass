Examining the Power Spectrum

Pick your target

Each target is a bright, Sun-like star that Kepler observed at one-minute cadence for months to years. Surface convection drives acoustic (p-mode) oscillations in these stars. You will find them, then measure their characteristic frequencies.

5 · Mass, radius and age from scaling relations

Turn your measurements into stellar properties with the scaling relations of Bellinger (2019, MNRAS 486, 4612).

The classical relations

Δν measures the star's mean density (Δν ∝ √(M/R³)) and νmax tracks the acoustic cut-off frequency (νmax ∝ g/√Teff ∝ M R−2 Teff−1/2). Solving those two proportionalities for M and R, relative to the Sun, gives:

\frac{M}{M_\odot} \simeq \left(\frac{\nu_\mathrm{max}}{\nu_{\mathrm{max},\odot}}\right)^{3}\left(\frac{\Delta\nu}{\Delta\nu_\odot}\right)^{-4}\left(\frac{T_\mathrm{eff}}{T_{\mathrm{eff},\odot}}\right)^{3/2}
\frac{R}{R_\odot} \simeq \left(\frac{\nu_\mathrm{max}}{\nu_{\mathrm{max},\odot}}\right)^{1}\left(\frac{\Delta\nu}{\Delta\nu_\odot}\right)^{-2}\left(\frac{T_\mathrm{eff}}{T_{\mathrm{eff},\odot}}\right)^{1/2}

Bellinger's calibrated relations

Bellinger keeps the same power-law form but lets the exponents float, and adds two more inputs: the small separation δν02, which is sensitive to the hydrogen left in the core (and so to age), and the metallicity. The exponents were fitted (by MCMC) to 80 Kepler main-sequence and early-subgiant stars whose masses, radii and ages had been found by fitting stellar models. Every relation has the form

\frac{Y}{Y_\odot} = \left(\frac{\nu_\mathrm{max}}{\nu_{\mathrm{max},\odot}}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\Delta\nu}{\Delta\nu_\odot}\right)^{\beta}\left(\frac{\delta\nu_{02}}{\delta\nu_{02,\odot}}\right)^{\gamma}\left(\frac{T_\mathrm{eff}}{T_{\mathrm{eff},\odot}}\right)^{\delta}\left(\frac{e^{[\mathrm{Fe/H}]}}{e^{[\mathrm{Fe/H}]_\odot}}\right)^{\varepsilon}

where Y is the star's mass, radius or age (Y⊙ = 1 M⊙, 1 R⊙ or 4.569 Gyr), and α, β, γ, δ and ε are exponents fitted separately for each of the three.

Solar reference values: νmax,⊙ = 3090 ± 30 µHz, Δν⊙ = 135.1 ± 0.1 µHz, δν02,⊙ = 8.957 ± 0.059 µHz, Teff,⊙ = 5772 ± 0.8 K, [Fe/H]⊙ = 0. The relations were calibrated on stars with Δν between 38 and 180 µHz and δν02 between 2.8 and 13 µHz. Outside that range (red giants especially) they are extrapolations.
